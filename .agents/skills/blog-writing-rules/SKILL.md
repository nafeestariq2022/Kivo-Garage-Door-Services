# Blog Writing Rules

House rules for every blog article. Structured for Google crawlability, featured-snippet eligibility, and easy LLM parsing. Follow all rules on every article.

## Article Length

- **Target 600 words exactly, give or take 10.** Acceptable range: 590-610 words.
- Count excludes headings, bylines, and metadata.
- Not a floor, not a ceiling. Hit the target. If a draft runs long, tighten it. If it runs short, add substance, not filler.

## Structure Requirements

Articles must be exceptionally structured for Google and LLM crawlers. Skimmable, scannable, chunked.

### Heading Hierarchy

- **H1** — article title (one per article, contains the target keyword).
- **H2** — main sections.
- **H3** — subsections inside H2s.
- **H4** — sub-subsections where needed for further breakdown.
- Use headings aggressively. Break content into many small named chunks rather than a few long ones.
- Every section heading should be a specific question or descriptive phrase, not a vague label.

### Paragraphs

- **Maximum a few sentences per paragraph.** Short. Tight.
- **Never stack multiple paragraphs in a row without structural breaks.** Between paragraphs, use a heading, a list, or a new subsection.
- If two paragraphs sit next to each other, one of them is probably a list or belongs under its own heading.

### Lists

- Use bulleted or numbered lists wherever content supports them: steps, factors, tiers, categories, requirements, examples, comparisons.
- Lists break up prose and help crawlers extract information cleanly.
- Listed items start with the same part of speech (parallel structure).

### Section Flow

- Heading → answer-first sentence → supporting detail (short paragraph or list) → next heading.
- No wall-of-text sections. If a section runs long, split it with an H3 or H4.

## Writing Rules

1. **Be Clear and Certain.** Factual statements, not hedging. "Bananas are rich in potassium." Not "Bananas might have some health benefits."
2. **Avoid Fluff.** No filler. Every sentence adds information.
3. **Use Specific Numbers.** Never "many" or "lots." Pair numbers with adjectives. "4 essential steps to reduce energy costs."
4. **Match Verbs to Context.** "Improve skills" / "Increase steps" — not the reverse.
5. **Provide Examples After Plural Nouns.** "3 essential vitamins like Vitamin C, D, and B12." Not "3 essential vitamins to consider."
6. **Consistent Sentence Structure.** Listed items start with the same part of speech.
7. **Optimize Subtext.** After a heading, the first sentence directly answers the heading.
8. **Answer Immediately.** The answer goes at the START of the first sentence. No preamble. No "Many people wonder…"
9. **Anchor Text Alignment.** Anchor text matches the target page's title or keyword. Never "click here" or "read more."
10. **Optimize for Search Engines and AI.** Use question-and-answer format where appropriate.
11. **Ask Unique Questions.** Section headings as specific questions. "What are the top 3 security risks for small business websites?" Not "Why is security important?"
12. **75/25 Rule.** 75% of the article answers the main topic. 25% covers related subtopics in 2-3 sentences each, each linking to a detailed resource.

## Formatting Rules

- **No bold in body copy.** Bold only in headings.
- **No em dashes in body copy.** Use commas, periods, or restructure the sentence.
- **No blank lines between sections.** Spacing is controlled through heading styles only.
- **Natural, contextual internal links.** See the Internal Linking section for counts and requirements.

## Internal Linking

Every article is a linking hub. Three layers of internal links are required on every post.

### 1. Blog → Related Blogs/Pages (3-5 links)

Include **3-5 internal links** to relevant blog posts or site pages from the client's own site. These build topical authority and keep users on-site.

- Anchor text must be keyword-focused, matching the target page's title or primary keyword.
- Links are woven naturally into the body copy, not dumped in a "related reading" block.
- When the related content is a full blog, you can add a short section with that blog's title as the heading, write 2-3 sentences about the topic, and link out with keyword-focused anchor text (exact title or close variation).

### 2. Blog → Service Pages or Product Pages (every relevant one)

Link to **every relevant service page or product page** from the client's site. The page type depends on the client's business model:

- **Service-based businesses** (law firms, agencies, payment processors, contractors): link to service pages.
- **Product-based businesses** (ecommerce, retail, manufacturers): link to product or category pages.
- **Hybrid businesses**: link to whichever page type matches the topic of the sentence.

Rules for these links:

- Anchor text must be keyword-focused and match the target page's title, H1, or primary keyword.
- Every relevant page gets its own link. If a blog about "best office chairs for back pain" reasonably touches on lumbar chairs, mesh chairs, and standing desks, all three product/category pages get linked.
- Parent service/product page link is mandatory (the blog's primary topic page).

### Anchor Text Rules

- Matches the target page's title, H1, or primary keyword exactly or as a close variation.
- Never generic phrases like "click here," "read more," "this article," "learn more," or "here."
- Never bare URLs.

## Pre-Publish Checklist

Before delivering an article, verify every item:

- [ ] 590-610 words (target 600, ±10)
- [ ] H1 contains target keyword
- [ ] H2/H3/H4 hierarchy used throughout
- [ ] No paragraph longer than a few sentences
- [ ] No two paragraphs stacked without a heading or list between them
- [ ] At least one list where content supports it
- [ ] Answer-first sentence after every heading
- [ ] No bold in body copy
- [ ] No em dashes in body copy
- [ ] 3-5 internal links to related blogs/pages on the client site
- [ ] Internal link to every relevant service page or product page
- [ ] Link back to parent service/product page (primary topic page)
- [ ] All anchor text keyword-focused, none generic
- [ ] Specific numbers used, no "many" or "lots"
- [ ] 75/25 main-topic / related-subtopic ratio
